## What happened

**The 9 altered scenarios.** Each takes the real, clean order 10250 and changes one thing. Where the change affects the invoice total, the total is recalculated so only the intended thing is wrong.

| Change | Code | What it protects against |
|---|---|---|
| No PO | `missing_po` | Paying for something nobody ordered |
| No receipt | `missing_receipt` | Paying before goods arrive |
| Extra product on the invoice | `not_on_po` + `not_received` | Padding an invoice with items |
| 12 billed, 10 ordered | `quantity_vs_po` + `quantity_vs_receipt` | Overbilling quantity |
| Apples price unreadable | `price_missing` + `total_mismatch` | Approving a line nobody could price |
| 45.00 billed, 42.40 agreed | `price_vs_po` | Overcharging |
| Receipt lacks a product | `not_received` | Paying for undelivered items |
| 30 of 35 arrived | `quantity_vs_receipt` | Paying for a short delivery |
| Total printed as 1900 | `total_mismatch` | Arithmetic errors or tampering |

Codes often come in pairs, because one real problem breaks two rules. An unreadable price also breaks the total check, since the matcher can only add up lines it can price (1813 − 35 × 42.4 = 329.0). Reporting every broken rule, not just the first, gives the person approving the full picture.

**Real order 11077: 13 exceptions, and two different causes.**

1. `missing_receipt` is real: the dataset has no shipping order for 11077 (the download returns 404).
2. `total_mismatch` is the dropped total from [step 01.8](01.8-crosscheck.md): the invoice total is `None`, the lines add up to 1374.60.
3. **The 11 `not_on_po` are a reading error, not a billing problem.** The PO has 2 pages. Nemotron parsed both, and page 2's table holds exactly those 11 products. But LLM extraction returned only page 1's 14 lines. Re-running the extraction twice without the cache gave 14 lines both times, so it is consistent, not bad luck.

**The design held:** the reading error made the case *fail*, never pass. A dropped line can only produce a false "not on PO", which holds the case for a person; it can't make a bad invoice look clean. That is the point of rules deciding money.

**But the error went unnoticed until matching.** The step 01.8 cross-check compares the text layer with the *parsed* text, and the parsed text was complete. Nothing yet compares the parsed text with the *extracted* `Document`. A guard for that is the obvious fix: count the line rows in the parsed tables, and if the extracted document has fewer, send the difference back to the LLM in the existing retry.

**Why it matters next:** Phase 2 is done; the matcher's behaviour is fully shown. Before or after Phase 3, the extraction gap above should be closed.
